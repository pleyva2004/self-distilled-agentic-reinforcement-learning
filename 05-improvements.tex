\noindent
This document proposes four concrete extensions to SDAR \citep{lu2026sdar}.  Each
section follows the same template: (a) statement, (b) prior-work anchoring,
(c) what would test it, (d) prototype pointer.  Notation matches the math
deep dive (\texttt{02-math-deep-dive.md}); in particular, $\Delta_t \;=\;
\log\pi_T(y_t\mid s_t^+)\!-\!\log\pi_\theta(y_t\mid s_t)$ is the
teacher--student gap, $g_t = \sigma(\beta\,\mathrm{score}_t)$ is the detached
gate, and the SDAR objective is
$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}(\theta) +
\lambda_{\mathrm{SDAR}}\cdot \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SDAR}}(\theta)$
with $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SDAR}} = \mathrm{Agg}(g_t \Delta_t)$.

\section*{1.\ Mathematical Improvements: a bias bound for gap gating}

\paragraph{Statement.}  Section~7 of the math deep dive sketches that
gap-gating reduces bias relative to ungated OPSD, but no closed form is
given.  We propose the following.

\begin{assumption}[Asymmetric reliability of the gap distribution]
\label{ass:asym}
Let $p(\Delta)$ denote the marginal distribution of $\Delta_t$ over training
tokens (under the joint trajectory distribution induced by $\pi_\theta$ and
the environment).  There exists a threshold $\tau \le 0$ such that the mass
$\int_{-\infty}^{\tau} p(\Delta)\,\mathrm{d}\Delta$ is dominated by
\emph{skill-retrieval failures} --- the privileged context $s_t^+$ contains
text that misled the teacher branch, so $\Delta < \tau$ is unreliable
distillation signal.  Mass with $\Delta \ge \tau$ corresponds to reliable
endorsement (or near-zero disagreement).
\end{assumption}

This is the formalisation of paper \S 1's three failure modes (skill quality,
skill utilisation, multi-turn drift), all of which manifest as
\emph{negative-tail} mass in $p(\Delta)$.

\begin{proposition}[Bias bound for the gated estimator]
\label{prop:bias}
Let $\hat g_{\text{ungated}}(\theta)
   = \mathbb{E}_{\Delta\sim p}[\Delta]$ be the per-token mean of the
ungated SDAR contribution and let
$\hat g_{\text{gated}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\Delta\sim p}[g(\Delta)\Delta]$
with $g(\Delta) = \sigma(\beta\Delta)$.  Define the \emph{ideal} estimator
$\hat g_{\text{true}}(\theta) = \int_{\tau}^{\infty}\Delta\,p(\Delta)\,
\mathrm{d}\Delta$ (the contribution restricted to reliable mass).  Then
\[
\bigl|\,\hat g_{\text{gated}}(\theta) - \hat g_{\text{true}}(\theta)\,\bigr|
\;\le\;
\underbrace{\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\!(1\!-\!g(\Delta))\,|\Delta|\,p(\Delta)\,
\mathrm{d}\Delta}_{\text{negative-tail leakage}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\!(1\!-\!g(\Delta))\,\Delta\,p(\Delta)\,
\mathrm{d}\Delta}_{\text{reliable-tail attenuation}}.
\]
Both terms vanish as $\beta\to\infty$ (hard step at zero), with rate
$O(\exp(-\beta|\tau|))$ for the first and $O(\beta^{-1})$ for the second.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Prior work.}  The decomposition mirrors the
baseline-doesn't-bias proof of GRPO \citep{shao2024deepseekmath} and the
chain Ch.~31 derivation, where a state-dependent baseline is shown to be
zero-mean against the policy gradient.  The gate $g_t$ is detached, so the
same conditional-expectation argument applies: gating shifts the
\emph{target} but does not introduce a new dependence on $\theta$.  The
asymmetric-tail framing is inspired by importance-sampling truncation
analyses common in PPO \citep{schulman2017ppo} and
InstructGPT-style RLHF \citep{ouyang2022instructgpt}.

\paragraph{What would test it.}  The full statement and proof appear in
\texttt{proofs/gating-bias-bound.tex}.  Empirically, the toy bandit in
\texttt{sandbox/toy\_sdar.py} (and the comparison in
\texttt{improvements/adaptive-gate.py}) lets us measure the empirical bias
of the gated vs.\ ungated estimator under a synthetic $p(\Delta)$ with
controllable negative-tail mass.

\paragraph{Prototype pointer.}  PROOF: \texttt{proofs/gating-bias-bound.tex}
(stub).  Numerical sanity check: \texttt{improvements/adaptive-gate.py}
already computes per-token gate-fire rates; extending to bias estimates is
$\sim$10 lines.

\section*{2.\ Code / Implementation Improvements: adaptive-quantile gating}

\paragraph{Statement.}  The paper uses a fixed sigmoid
$g_t = \sigma(\beta\,\Delta_t)$ centred at $\Delta_t = 0$.  We propose
\[
   g_t \;=\; \sigma\bigl(\beta\,(\Delta_t - \hat q_\tau)\bigr),
   \qquad
   \hat q_\tau \leftarrow (1-\eta)\,\hat q_\tau + \eta\,\Delta_t^{\text{thresh}},
\]
where $\hat q_\tau$ is an exponentially-moving estimate of the
$\tau$-quantile (e.g.\ $\tau=0.3$) of recent gaps.  Rationale: the gap
distribution drifts over training as the student policy improves; the
\emph{absolute} criterion $\Delta_t > 0$ stops being meaningful after a few
hundred steps because typical gaps shift toward zero.  A relative criterion
``fire on the top $1\!-\!\tau$ fraction'' stays well-calibrated.

\paragraph{Prior work.}  Quantile-based truncation has a long history in
robust statistics; the closest deep-RL precedent is
\textsc{V-Trace}-style importance-clipping in IMPALA, and the
running-quantile baselines in \citet{wang2026tcod}.  The detached-gate
structure is preserved, so the convergence properties argued in Prop.~1
still apply.

\paragraph{What would test it.}  Three-way comparison on the toy MDP:
GRPO baseline, fixed-threshold SDAR ($\sigma(2\Delta_t)$), and
adaptive-quantile SDAR.  Metric: final reward subject to a per-turn KL
budget identical to the paper's stability criterion.

\paragraph{Prototype pointer.}  MEASUREMENT:
\texttt{improvements/adaptive-gate.py}.  Returns
\verb|{fixed_threshold_reward, adaptive_threshold_reward, fixed_kl,|
\verb|adaptive_kl, gate_fire_rate_fixed, gate_fire_rate_adaptive}|.

\section*{3.\ Experimental Extensions: $\lambda_{\mathrm{SDAR}}$ sensitivity sweep}

\paragraph{Statement.}  The SDAR ablation in the paper fixes
$\lambda_{\mathrm{SDAR}}$ at one value and varies the gating strategy.  But
$\lambda_{\mathrm{SDAR}}$ is itself a critical hyperparameter:
$\lambda_{\mathrm{SDAR}}=0$ recovers GRPO and gives no distillation;
$\lambda_{\mathrm{SDAR}}\to\infty$ overwhelms the RL signal and the policy
collapses to a maximum-likelihood estimate of the teacher.  Somewhere
between is an optimum.  We propose a 5-point sweep
$\lambda_{\mathrm{SDAR}} \in \{0,\,0.1,\,0.3,\,1,\,3\}$ at fixed $\beta$,
reporting both final reward and final per-turn KL, and identifying the
constrained optimum
\[
   \lambda^\star \;=\; \arg\max_\lambda \mathrm{reward}(\lambda)
   \quad \text{s.t.\ } \mathrm{kl}(\lambda)\le 2\cdot\mathrm{kl}(0).
\]

\paragraph{Prior work.}  Standard auxiliary-loss-weight sweeps appear in
\citet{deepseek2025r1} and \citet{zhao2026opsd}; both observe an inverted-U
in reward as the auxiliary weight grows.  Our sweep is the
agentic-RL specialisation of this exercise.

\paragraph{What would test it.}  Five 100-step training runs on the toy
MDP, one per $\lambda$ value, with $\beta=2$ held constant.  Constrained
$\arg\max$ over the resulting (reward, KL) Pareto trace.

\paragraph{Prototype pointer.}  MEASUREMENT:
\texttt{improvements/lambda-sweep.py}.  Returns the full
\verb|{sweep, optimal_lambda}| dictionary.

\section*{4.\ Theoretical / Conceptual Connections: SDAR as constrained RL}

\paragraph{Statement.}  Frame the SDAR objective as a
\emph{constrained} GRPO problem with a per-token trust constraint:
\[
   \max_\theta \; J_{\mathrm{GRPO}}(\theta)
   \quad \text{s.t.\ } \;
   \mathbb{E}_t \bigl[\,\mathbf{1}[t\in\mathcal{T}]\;
        D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s_t)\,\|\,\pi_T(\cdot\mid s_t^+))
   \bigr] \;\le\; \delta,
\]
where $\mathcal{T}$ is the set of \emph{trusted} tokens (those for which
the privileged context endorses the student trajectory).  The Lagrangian is
\[
   \mathcal{L}(\theta,\mu)
   \;=\; J_{\mathrm{GRPO}}(\theta) \;-\; \mu\cdot
        \Bigl(\mathbb{E}_t[\mathbf{1}[t\in\mathcal{T}]\cdot
              D_{\mathrm{KL}}^{(t)}] - \delta\Bigr).
\]
SDAR's gated loss has \emph{exactly} this structure with the global
multiplier $\mu$ replaced by a state-dependent per-token multiplier
$\lambda_{\mathrm{SDAR}}\cdot g_t$.  In other words, SDAR is a
\emph{soft, primal-only} approximation to a per-token trust-constrained RL
problem, where the gate $g_t$ is an indicator surrogate for membership in
$\mathcal{T}$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{SDAR}}$ is a fixed multiplier that
plays the role of the dual variable.

\paragraph{Prior work.}  Constrained RL with primal-dual saddle-point
solvers traces back to the option/sub-goal formalism of
\citet{sutton1999options}.  The chain Ch.~30 max-entropy-RL derivation
gives the same Lagrangian form for the entropy-regularised case.  The
modern primal-only variant (treating $\mu$ as a hyperparameter rather than
a learned dual variable) is the same shortcut taken by PPO's KL-penalty
schedule \citep{schulman2017ppo}.

\paragraph{What would test it.}  This is a translation, not a new theorem.
The deliverable is a short proof sketch in
\texttt{proofs/sdar-as-constrained-rl.tex} that (i) writes the SDAR
gradient as the gradient of the Lagrangian above with $\mu = \lambda_{\mathrm{SDAR}}$
and the indicator replaced by $g_t$, and (ii) cites the saddle-point
convergence conditions from constrained-RL literature for which SDAR's
fixed multiplier is sound (essentially: when the constraint is slack at
the optimum, primal-only with a sufficiently large $\mu$ works).

\paragraph{Prototype pointer.}  PROOF:
\texttt{proofs/sdar-as-constrained-rl.tex} (stub).

\section*{Discussion}

The four improvements compose.  The mathematical bias bound (\S 1) is the
\emph{justification} for adaptive-quantile gating (\S 2): the bound's
negative-tail leakage term shrinks faster when the gate centre tracks the
empirical $\tau$-quantile rather than sitting at zero.  The
$\lambda_{\mathrm{SDAR}}$ sweep (\S 3), once run jointly with the adaptive
gate, would reveal a \emph{joint} optimum
$(\lambda^\star, \tau^\star)$, plausibly different from the
single-variable optimum.  And the constrained-RL framing (\S 4) gives the
$\lambda_{\mathrm{SDAR}}$ sweep its theoretical reading: the optimal
$\lambda^\star$ is an estimate of the dual variable that would solve the
constrained problem at the desired KL budget.  Together they upgrade
SDAR from a heuristic auxiliary loss to a principled primal approximation
of a per-token trust-constrained agentic-RL problem.
