\section*{Background: Multi-Turn Agentic Post-Training}

Modern post-training of LLM agents is dominated by reinforcement learning
with verifiable rewards. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO),
introduced by \citet{shao2024deepseekmath} and scaled by
\citet{deepseek2025r1}, is now the workhorse: it strips the value head
from PPO \citep{schulman2017ppo} and replaces the GAE baseline with a
group-mean baseline computed across $G$ rollouts of the same prompt. The
trajectory-level reward is then broadcast token-wise via a clipped
importance ratio. This pipeline is conceptually a descendant of RLHF
\citep{christiano2017deep, ouyang2022instructgpt}, with the human-preference
reward model swapped for a programmatic verifier.

The structural weakness of GRPO in agentic settings is supervisory
sparsity. A 20-turn trajectory in ALFWorld \citep{shridhar2020alfworld}
or WebShop \citep{yao2022webshop} compresses thousands of token-level
decisions into a single scalar reward. \emph{On-Policy Distillation}
\citep{ye2026opd, yang2026opd} and its self-distilled variant
\emph{OPSD} \citep{zhao2026opsd} address this by adding a per-token
auxiliary loss against a teacher branch. In the self-distilled regime
the teacher is the \emph{same} policy conditioned on a privileged context
$s_t^+ = s_t \cup \text{(retrieved skill text)}$ available only at training
time --- a free dense signal. SDAR \citep{lu2026sdar} is the agentic
extension of this idea, plus a sigmoid gate to handle the multi-turn
failure modes documented below.

\section*{Method: SDAR}

The full objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta) \;=\; \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GRPO}}(\theta)
       \;+\; \lambda_{\mathrm{SDAR}}\cdot \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SDAR}}(\theta),
\label{eq:sdar-objective}
\end{equation}
with the per-token SDAR loss
\begin{equation}
\ell_t^{\mathrm{SDAR}} \;=\; g_t \cdot \big(\log\pi_T(y_t\mid s_t^+) - \log\pi_\theta(y_t\mid s_t)\big)
       \;=\; g_t\cdot \Delta_t,
\label{eq:sdar-token}
\end{equation}
aggregated by masked-token average,
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SDAR}} = \big(\sum_t m_t g_t \Delta_t\big) / \big(\sum_t m_t\big)$.
The gap $\Delta_t$ is exactly the negative single-sample estimator of the
per-token reverse KL between teacher and student, evaluated at the
student-sampled token --- avoiding a full $|\mathcal{V}|$-wide softmax sum.

\citet{lu2026sdar} benchmark three gating strategies:
\begin{align}
\text{entropy:}  \quad & g_t = \sigma(\beta\, h_t), \\
\text{gap:}      \quad & g_t = \sigma(\beta\, \Delta_t), \\
\text{soft-OR:}  \quad & g_t = \sigma\!\big(\beta\,[1 - (1-h_t)(1-\Delta_t)]\big).
\end{align}
Gap gating is the conceptual centerpiece: it operationalises the
asymmetric-trust observation. When $\Delta_t > 0$ the privileged context
is endorsing the student's sampled token --- high-value distillation
signal --- and the gate opens. When $\Delta_t < 0$ the disagreement is
ambiguous (could be skill-retrieval failure rather than a true student
error), and the sigmoid attenuates the update. The gate is detached, so
the gradient flows only through the student log-prob:
\begin{equation}
\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SDAR}}
  \;=\; -\,\mathrm{Agg}\!\left[\,g_t\cdot \nabla_\theta \log\pi_\theta(y_t\mid s_t)\,\right],
\end{equation}
which is structurally a gated REINFORCE-style update with $g_t$ as a
per-token signed weight.

support for the multi-turn-instability story.

\section*{Position in the Literature}

\paragraph{RL backbone.} SDAR's GRPO term is taken essentially verbatim
from \citet{shao2024deepseekmath}. The PPO surrogate
\citep{schulman2017ppo} is therefore the deepest underlying optimisation
primitive --- with the value head removed and the GAE baseline replaced
by a group-mean baseline. SDAR contributes \emph{nothing} to the RL
backbone itself; its novelty is the auxiliary objective.

\paragraph{Distillation lineage.} The auxiliary term descends from On-Policy
Distillation \citep{ye2026opd, yang2026opd}, which formalised teacher
guidance on student-sampled trajectories, and from On-Policy
Self-Distillation \citep{zhao2026opsd}, which collapses teacher and
student into a single set of parameters with a privileged-context
teacher branch. SDAR's contribution to this lineage is two-fold: (i) the
agentic, multi-turn observation that vanilla OPSD diverges, and (ii) the
gating remedy.

\paragraph{Curriculum vs gating.} A parallel response to multi-turn OPSD
instability is the turn-level curriculum of TCOD \citep{wang2026tcod},
which schedules teacher influence by turn index. SDAR's gating is the
``smooth'' alternative --- the gate is computed per token from the
realised teacher--student gap rather than scheduled exogenously by turn.
The two designs are not mutually exclusive; combining them is an obvious
follow-up.

\paragraph{RLHF foundations.} Conceptually the gated distillation term
plays a role analogous to the KL-to-reference regulariser in InstructGPT
\citep{ouyang2022instructgpt} and the broader RLHF programme
\citep{christiano2017deep}: both prevent the policy from drifting too
far from a reference distribution. SDAR replaces the static SFT
reference with a dynamic privileged-context teacher and gates the
constraint per-token rather than treating it as a fixed Lagrangian.

\paragraph{Sibling agentic-RL work.} \citet{rlm2026} (the
``Reinforcing Recursive Language Models'' alphaXiv blog from the
NovaSky-AI / SkyRL group) attacks the same supervisory-sparsity problem
from a different angle: a shared policy is rolled out across a
\emph{recursive call tree}, and child rollouts inherit advantages from
their parent. Credit assignment is by tree topology rather than by
per-token reliability gating. The two designs are complementary;
\S\,Discussion sketches a hybrid.

\paragraph{Benchmarks.} The agentic suite is standard:
ALFWorld \citep{shridhar2020alfworld}, WebShop
\citep{yao2022webshop}, and Search-QA-style tasks
\citep{searchqa2017}. None of these were designed to stress 50+-turn
horizons, which is one reason the depth-scaling question (below)
remains open.

\section*{Discussion}

\paragraph{What the experiments support.} Three claims are well-supported
by Table 1 and Figure 2 of \citet{lu2026sdar}: (a) ungated OPSD on top of
GRPO destabilises multi-turn training; (b) any of the three proposed
gates restores stability; (c) gated SDAR strictly improves over the GRPO
baseline on every reported aggregate metric for both 3B and 7B
backbones. The KL-bounded behaviour shown in Figure 2 is direct evidence
for the failure-mode analysis in \S\,1 of the paper.

\paragraph{What is hand-waved.} The bias--variance argument for gating
is informal in the paper --- there is no formal theorem that
gap-gated OPSD is a lower-variance estimator than ungated OPSD, only an
empirical demonstration. Sensitivity to the two new hyperparameters
($\beta$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{SDAR}}$) is not ablated in the main text.
The interpretation of $\Delta_t$ as a ``trust'' signal also presupposes
that teacher log-prob is a reliable proxy for skill-retrieval quality
--- a confidently wrong teacher (high $\Delta_t$, low actual quality)
would be heavily distilled into the student. This is plausible but not
directly tested; the slight under-performance of SDAR vs GRPO+OPSD on
some Search-QA sub-tasks is consistent with this critique.

\paragraph{Three future directions.}
(1)~\emph{Depth scaling:} re-run on a benchmark with 50--100 effective
turns and check whether the per-turn KL stays bounded. (2)~\emph{Adaptive
gates:} learn $\beta$ jointly with $\theta$, or replace the
hand-designed gate with $g_\phi(s_t, \Delta_t, h_t)$. (3)~\emph{Recursive-call
extension:} lift gating into the recursive setting of \citet{rlm2026}
--- gate child rollouts by whether the parent endorses them, combining
SDAR-style per-token gating with RLM-style tree-level credit
assignment.
